PSVR2-DriverKit

Native PlayStation VR2 Support for Linux

Use the Sony PlayStation VR2 and its Sense controllers on Linux, with SteamVR games and OpenXR apps. It's an open-source driver based on Monado, the open-source OpenXR runtime, and it's native: no Windows driver or Wine underneath. A desktop app installs, compiles and launches everything.

What you get

Native SteamVR integration Stable

A SteamVR driver registered with Steam: SteamVR starts with the PS VR2 like any headset, from the Library or a game.

SteamVR on Linux

OpenXR support Stable

Native Linux OpenXR apps run on Monado's runtime without SteamVR, on the same PS VR2 drivers.

OpenXR with Monado

Headset & controller tracking Stable

6DoF head tracking and 6DoF Sense controllers: the headset's cameras track the controllers' LEDs, fused with their motion sensors.

Sense controllers

Eye tracking Works

Gaze for SteamVR games and for OpenXR apps (XR_EXT_eye_gaze_interaction). No per-user calibration yet.

Eye tracking

Passthrough Stable

SteamVR Room View from the headset's cameras, toggled with the headset's function button, plus the camera near your play-area walls.

Passthrough

Haptics & every input Stable

All buttons, sticks and triggers, capacitive touch, finger-distance sensors, vibration, and a finger skeleton for games that animate hands.

Controller details

Wayland integration Tested on GNOME

Works on Wayland compositors that lease the headset's display. Your desktop in VR works through WayVR.

Wayland & X11

GUI management Stable

The PSVR2-DriverKit app installs packages and udev rules, compiles the driver, registers it with Steam, and launches it with pre-flight checks.

Installation

Experimental

Bare-hand tracking Experimental

Hands tracked by the headset's cameras with Monado's Mercury tracker while a controller is off. Off by default; needs a driver compiled with hand tracking.

Desktop in VR (WayVR) Works

Starts the separate WayVR overlay with SteamVR (or Monado), with Sense controller bindings included. Off by default.

What you need

  • The PS VR2 with Sony's PS VR2 PC adapter, and a GPU with a free DisplayPort output.
  • A Linux PC with Steam (native package) and SteamVR. Arch-, Debian- and Fedora-based distributions get automatic package installation.
  • Sense controllers paired over Bluetooth.

Tested on NVIDIA with GNOME Wayland; Intel Arc runs without async reprojection; AMD is expected but not yet reported. See compatibility.
